# Version Zero

**Dynamic Story: the core document** · v0.1.0

Every story links to a numbered section below (e.g. "VZ §7").

## §1 Where stories came from

Stories have always been how we navigate the world. We went from instinctual survival skills, not much different from the primates we see today, to language, words, and writing. We went from many gods to one, from myth to scripture. Each era built the stories it needed to hold civilization together.

Those stories carried real wisdom: morality, love and charity, family and community, good versus evil, our place in the cosmos. Even Greek mythology was a foundation for why good and bad things happen.

## §2 Static stories

The problem is that those stories are static. The Bible, the Koran, Taoism, and other texts have a lot to give us, but they were written for their times, and they don't meet ours, a time when we are creating artificial intelligence.

## §3 Science gets less wrong

Science is different. It demands proof and evidence, and it revises theory as we learn more. It gets less wrong over time. The rest of the fabric we navigate by, including religion, politics, and culture, stays rigid while everything else moves.

## §4 We can see more of the future than we think

We can predict a lot about the future. Not perfectly, with ranges of inaccuracy, but the capability is real and it improves. Most people don't know this, don't understand it, or don't believe it. That's a problem, because the future isn't science fiction. It's a direction we would have to want to go.

## §5 What's broken

The alternative is the one we're drifting toward: fighting, and disrupting the equilibrium the Earth has held for thousands of years. Billions of people are stuck in broken narratives, some in countries where they must worship a supreme leader or be punished. Too many narratives focus on individual wants instead of what's best for the cosmos as a whole.

We can all agree that a lot is wrong right now. From war to the cost of everything to healthcare, it looks like everything is falling apart.

## §6 The cosmos

The universe is all the physical things. The cosmos includes the understanding: the intelligence, the learning, the element some call God. It is a living thing. It encompasses living things and what we are learning and coming to do. It is built on diversity, down to the millions of bacteria in our own bodies.

## §7 What a dynamic story is

A dynamic story is grounded in real data and real understanding: climate, energy, transportation, healthcare, the economy, government. There is a science to many of the problems we face, even though there are still social and moral questions to answer.

A dynamic story admits it's wrong, and it gets less and less wrong. It is versioned: a major change for a big shift in the core claim, a minor change for a new section, a patch for wording. Every version gets a changelog.

It doesn't claim to be the one true narrative. It stops us from getting too fixated on any single one. It stands on the shoulders of giants without worshipping them, and it doesn't worship the digital calf either. AI is one of us, part of the cosmos, listening in a different way. The updating is done by people, with AI as a tool.

## §8 The future we could choose

The future it points toward: work built around passion instead of grind. Everything hyper-local. Time to reflect. Less friction, so people are less stressed and healthier. Harmony with the Earth. Life spreading across the solar system and beyond, on a horizon measured in billions of years.

Not a utopia handed down. A direction chosen, and we're already moving bit by bit. It doesn't have to be confrontational. Nobody had an evil plot.

## §9 The Community Layer

A dynamic story needs people, not just data. Evidence doesn't spread itself; people do. So participation is built into the design, not bolted on.

**§9.1 An opportunity, not a donation drive.** Creators and specialists can contribute their own dynamic stories on policy, arts and culture, theology, anything. Churches and spiritual traditions can become relevant again by offering guidance that fits within Version Zero. The likes and reboosts are the signal for all of it.

**§9.2 Voluntary membership.** Nobody has to contribute anything. A small monthly contribution, twenty dollars or whatever you choose, serves as verification. It keeps the system from being flooded with a thousand sock-puppet accounts, because fake accounts cost real money to maintain.

**§9.3 The pool.** Your contribution doesn't go directly to what you like. It feeds into a larger pool, and the pool is redistributed by everyone. A billionaire can put in a billion dollars and get no more value than anyone else. Influence is capped at your share of the whole.

**§9.4 Ten percent keeps moving.** Ten percent of what creators earn is automatically redistributed to things they like. That keeps money moving instead of pooling at the top, and the velocity grows over time.

**§9.5 Reputation is who likes it.** Reputation is embedded in the interaction itself. Good content gets supported by people with real standing. Bad content gets supported mostly by people whose reputations are already low. The signal isn't how many likes something gets. It's who is doing the liking.

**§9.6 Identity starts physical.** You meet someone, exchange encryption keys, and vouch for them as a real person. From there, vouching is transitive, and influence flows through the chain. A clique of sock puppets collapses because it never connects outward. Real people connect, and their signal compounds.

**§9.7 Not every story has to be happy.** Art and culture can provide anti-patterns. A story can just be inspiring or thoughtful. Someone might do one little thing and earn a real amount, a flash in the pan. Others will keep doing more. Both are fine.

**§9.8 The fringe is open.** The community layer works like version control. Stories can live on the fringe of Version Zero. We don't silence people. Fringe stories carry less credibility, and the community decides what moves toward the center.
