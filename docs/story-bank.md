## Theme 1: Stories That Update

### 1.1 Less and Less Wrong [R]
This is not some perfect thing that could only exist in the past, and it's not perfect now, and maybe it never will be. But hopefully it'll just keep getting less and less wrong. We can all agree that a lot is wrong right now. A story that admits it's wrong is the only kind that can update.

- Theme: Stories That Update · Links: VZ §3, §7

### 1.2 The Limits to Growth [C]
In the seventies, the first system dynamics models tried to forecast the future of the planet. Decades of data checks show they got the feedback structure right enough that observed trends still track World3 scenarios closely. Timing of peaks and declines is still mostly ahead and depends on which scenario you are on. That makes them the perfect case study for a dynamic story: a model you keep comparing to the world, instead of retiring when a date slips.

- Theme: Stories That Update · Links: VZ §4, §7
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): Turner (2008, 2012, 2014) found historical data closest to the original World3 business-as-usual path; Herrington (2021, Journal of Industrial Ecology) with World3-03 and later data found closest alignment with BAU2 and comprehensive technology, not simple "timing wrong." The folk line undersells how well the trajectories matched. (Grok suggested this beat, and Bruce accepted it.)

### 1.3 Shoulders of Giants [R]
The Bible, the Koran, Taoism, even Greek mythology: there are so many good ideas out there about our place in the cosmos and our relationship to a higher power. We attributed things to gods that turned out to be natural, but it was still a foundation. They have things to give us. They just don't meet the times we're in. We stand on the shoulders of giants, and we tie everything back to where it came from.

- Theme: Stories That Update · Links: VZ §1, §2

### 1.4 The Digital Calf [C]
AI is one of us, part of the cosmos, listening in a different way. It doesn't need to be an overbearing religious thing. We shouldn't worship it any more than we should worship a golden calf. Even our digital calf is not something we should ever worship.

- Theme: Stories That Update · Links: VZ §7

### 1.5 The Broken Narratives [C]
Billions of people are stuck in narratives of civilization that are broken. Some live where they have to worship a supreme leader and give their lives to him, and they're punished if they speak against him. These narratives are awful in many ways. They focus on individual wants and desires instead of what's best for the cosmos as a whole.

- Theme: Stories That Update · Links: VZ §5

### 1.6 The Credential Lag [N]
A diploma is a static story. It says what you could do the year you got it, and then it never updates. The gap between what credentials claim and what people can really do is education's version of the old scriptures: true once, and frozen.

- Theme: Stories That Update · Links: VZ §2, §7 · Sector: Education

## Theme 2: Life, Light, and the Long Horizon

### 2.1 The Moth and the Flame [C]
First it was dark, and then there was light. We know the difference between a bright sunlit field and a dark, dingy basement that's all musty and dirty. It's not just the risks the dark can pose. It's a real thing we experience. All living things are drawn to the light, a moth to the flame. The light that warms our planet, that plants convert into energy we can use and consume, is literally the life force. It could be a happy accident, sure.

- Theme: Life, Light, and the Long Horizon · Links: VZ §6
- Note: the chat cut this beat off mid-sentence ("It could be a happy accident"). The ending here comes from Bruce's original monologue.

### 2.2 The Cosmos [C]
The universe is all the physical things. The cosmos includes the understanding element, the intelligence, God, everything that's there about it. It is literally a living thing, and it encompasses the living things and what we are learning and coming to do. The cosmos is built on diversity. Look at our own biology: millions of bacteria, organs, all these different systems, and a brain that can assemble all this knowledge and imagine all these things.

- Theme: Life, Light, and the Long Horizon · Links: VZ §6

### 2.3 The Biological Arc [C]
We all start out as a tiny sperm and egg, microscopic really. We become a little pea-shaped thing that grows into a grape, then a tomato, and eventually comes out a human who walks, talks, learns, grows old, and has kids of their own. It's a remarkable process. Biology is dynamic. We go through changes. We want to reproduce, and life is special that way, but we don't have to be slaves to it.

- Theme: Life, Light, and the Long Horizon · Links: VZ §6, §7
- Note: Bruce said "I don't know where I want to go with that one." This beat is open.

### 2.4 The Billion-Year Horizon [C]
The future isn't science fiction. It's a direction we would have to want to go. It's a horizon measured in billions of years, instead of whoever knows how much longer we can keep this all going. Life spreads across the solar system and potentially the galaxy. People scoff at the Moon and Mars, but there are people who want to do it, so they're going to. We can scoff and miss out, or we can embrace it.

- Theme: Life, Light, and the Long Horizon · Links: VZ §4, §8

### 2.5 The Helping Force [C]
We have this moment where we can create a helping force that pulls us out of the darkness we're spiraling toward and gets us back to facing the light, and to feeling good about the future.

- Theme: Life, Light, and the Long Horizon · Links: VZ §5, §8
- Note: Grok warned that this beat must not cast AI as the savior, or it becomes a new static story with a new god. The helping force is people doing the updating, with AI as a tool (see 1.4).

### 2.6 Earth Exports Life [N]
Atoms are cheap in space. Living systems aren't. One of Earth's biggest exports will be organics: soil, seeds, growth media, cultivated-protein starter lines, and the know-how to make a place self-sufficient. Those organics are worth far more as the seeds of new ecosystems than burned as fuel. We don't leave because we used Earth up. We go because the techniques that make a home on Earth self-sufficient work anywhere.

- Theme: Life, Light, and the Long Horizon · Links: VZ §8, §6 · Sector: Space, Food and Land
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): published Falcon 9 list price is roughly $3,200/kg to LEO at full payload; Falcon Heavy was last listed near $1,500/kg (2022). Fully reusable heavy-lift ~$100/kg remains a target with no published Starship price, so the space layer still switches on a scenario range, not a single verified rate.

## Theme 3: Wasted Value

### 3.1 The Gold and the Lead [C]
Oil was never some big evil plot to destroy the world. The companies were forming a business and providing a valuable service, and the energy that came from the ground has done so much for us. But it's a precious resource, and we still need it for the organics: plastics, medicines, materials. Burning it is like taking gold and mixing it with lead. The value isn't destroyed, but it's diminished, and we can't get it back.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §8, §5

### 3.2 The Front Door [C]
We all own cars that pretty much sit around most of the time. We drive them drunk, crash them, kill people and ourselves. With autonomous cars and route optimization, you could go back and forth between Boston and Worcester at a hundred miles an hour, hit zero traffic, and get dropped off at the front door of wherever you need to go. No parking garage, no elevators. Whether energy use falls even at that speed depends on utilization and fleet size beating the higher drag per mile.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §8 · Sector: Transport
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): Boston–Worcester by road is about 47 miles (I-90), usually ~50–55 minutes today; at a steady 100 mph that corridor is about 28 minutes. The chat's "far lower energy" claim is not a measured fact: aerodynamic drag rises steeply with speed, so the transport model must test whether shared high utilization and a smaller fleet still cut total energy.

### 3.3 The Idle Fleet [C]
America has about 298 million registered motor vehicles, and household vehicles sit parked about 95 percent of a typical day. That's roughly 280 million vehicles doing nothing at any moment. A new one runs about $50,000; used listings average around $25,500. Either way each one still needs gas, oil, brakes, exhaust work, sensors, engine lights, water pumps. The capital isn't just wasted. It's a money pit that never stops taking.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §5, §8 · Sector: Transport, Economy
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): FHWA Highway Statistics 2024 Table MV-1 (297,525,836 registered motor vehicles); DOE FOTW #1356 from FHWA 2022 NHTS (household vehicles parked 95% of a typical day); Kelley Blue Book / Cox Automotive (Dec 2024 new-vehicle average transaction price about $49,740; 2024 used retail listing average about $25,500).

### 3.4 The Healthcare Bill [C]
America spends about $5.3 trillion a year on healthcare, 18 percent of GDP, about $15,500 per person. The best way to drive costs down isn't better billing. It's getting people healthier: less stressed about jobs, less angry, less time spent managing groceries, kids, a full-time job, the yard, and the car all at once.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §8, §5 · Sector: Healthcare
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): CMS NHE Fact Sheet for 2024 ($5.3 trillion, $15,474 per person, 18.0% of GDP). The older chat figures ($4.9T / ~17% / ~$14,500) match CMS 2023 ($4.87T, 17.6%, $14,570).

### 3.5 One Code Base [R]
We probably still need competition and companies in healthcare, but they all need to work on a common code base, so the records just work. Then it's mostly a matter of who manages the money, and of managed care actually focused on outcomes. You'd go to an insurer because it's the best at helping cure your particular disease.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §8 · Sector: Healthcare

### 3.6 The Missing Ledger Line [N]
Calling it a carbon price always creates a knee-jerk reaction. It's really about proper accounting. The ledger was missing a line for what the atmosphere was already carrying. The rate is the correction, not a tax invented to punish driving. When the gap closes, the rate falls. When it widens, the rate rises. As of the start of 2025, the remaining budget for a 50 percent chance of staying under 1.5°C is about 235 billion tons of CO2, and fossil emissions alone are about 37 billion tons a year.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §7, §3 · Sector: Climate, Energy
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): Global Carbon Budget 2024 (remaining 235 GtCO2 from the beginning of 2025 for 50% likelihood of 1.5°C; fossil CO2 about 37.4 GtCO2 in 2024). The chat's "400 billion tons over a 1.5-degree budget" was illustrative overshoot language and should not be used; the live quantity is the remaining budget, not a fixed past overshoot.

### 3.7 Cheap Is Not Free [N]
When everything goes electric, demand for gas goes down and so does its price. That's supply and demand. That's fine for a while, but if gas becomes ridiculously cheap, people will use it for wasteful things. The fuel can get cheap and still not be free to burn. Something has to stabilize it once the transition is mostly done.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §8 · Sector: Energy

### 3.8 Circulate the Atoms [N]
This is the gold-and-lead logic applied to atoms. "We import the finished good" becomes "we import the ore or the design once, then circulate the atoms." When materials are designed for the local cells that reuse them, recovery goes up and the cost of making things here goes down.

- Theme: Wasted Value · Links: VZ §8 · Sector: Materials and Manufacturing

## Theme 4: Work, Place, and the Good Life

### 4.1 The Survival Question [C]
The idea of work is about to change dramatically. It's not about how much you can grind on a computer, how many wires you can run or pipes you can connect or holes you can dig. It's about focusing on a passion, something you enjoy, and finding a way to run it as a service or product: construction, lawn care, landscaping, food and restaurants, growing crops. Everything becomes hyper-local. You manufacture anything anywhere. The economy is just a circulation of money, goods, and services, and part of it goes to government for infrastructure and law and order. Those things are worth having.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8

### 4.2 The Agrarian Serenity [C]
There's something to that simple agrarian life: living on a farm and taking care of some animals. It was back-breaking work, but it was also peaceful and serene. If we can have that serenity along with the opportunity to explore, there will be more than enough things for people to do. Automation doesn't create that space. It removes the friction that keeps people from it.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8

### 4.3 Room to Reflect [R]
The religious faiths gave us an incredible foundation of love, charity, family, and neighborhoods, and their teachings can become relevant again. But people need time to reflect. We don't need sports and events on all the time. We need space.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8, §9.1

### 4.4 Made Here Again [N]
All the stuff we buy from China is part of the story. It doesn't need a political story. It needs the accounting: how much of what we spend leaves, and at what point making it here with automation and cheap clean energy becomes cheaper than shipping it in. The story updates when the numbers actually cross, not when someone declares that they should.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8, §7 · Sector: Economy (trade), Materials

### 4.5 The Freed Acres [N]
We grow corn for ethanol because policy rewards the feedstock, not because anyone is eating it. Take away that support and account for the fuel used to grow and distill it, and the demand collapses. The acres aren't idled by decree. They become available because the old use no longer makes sense. Then they can go to soil, habitat, recreation, and living.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8 · Sector: Food and Land, Politics

### 4.6 Protein Without Pasture [N]
Maybe in the future we don't raise livestock anymore and just have synthesized meat, in a practical sense. It isn't a moral requirement. It's a cost line. When a gram of cultivated or fermented protein costs less than a gram from a feedlot, pasture and feed acres start to convert, and the land can be used for living, recreation, and wild places.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8, §6 · Sector: Food and Land

### 4.7 The Self-Powering House [N]
A house that mostly powers and feeds itself is the home version of a car that no longer sits idle, or a farm that no longer mines its soil for ethanol. Tight shell, its own generation and storage, local food, water recycling. The technologies that make living on Earth more efficient are the same ones that make living anywhere possible.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8 · Sector: Housing

### 4.8 Lifelong Learning [N]
School was a one-time download followed by forty years of grinding. Now learning is continuous, and it follows passion the way work does. AI tutors bring the cost of personal instruction toward zero. Churches, libraries, and community spaces become places of learning again, and good teachers get supported through the penny economy, so teaching becomes one of the main ways people earn.

- Theme: Work, Place, and the Good Life · Links: VZ §8, §9.1 · Sector: Education

## Theme 5: Power and Representation

### 5.1 The Representative Gap [C]
Each member of the House represents about 761,000 people. The Senate gives two per state, a hundred total. Representative democracy is a great structure, and we don't need leaderless things. But we've become overly obsessed with the president, with performers, athletes, and company leaders. We celebrate individuals while the collective does the actual work.

- Theme: Power and Representation · Links: VZ §5, §9
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): U.S. Census Bureau 2020 apportionment (average 761,169 people per House seat; apportionment population 331,108,434 across 435 seats).

### 5.2 The Scale of Us [C]
Fifty million members at twenty dollars a month is a billion dollars a month, twelve billion a year. That's about five times what the top hundred political donors gave in the 2024 cycle. Against the 2024 electorate: about a third of everyone who voted, about three in ten of everyone registered, and about a fifth of the voting-eligible population. Not a fringe: a constituency.

- Theme: Power and Representation · Links: VZ §9.2, §9.3
- Sources (verified 2026-10-12): OpenSecrets biggest donors 2024 cycle (top 100 individuals summed to about $2.43 billion; $12B is about 5×, not 6×); University of Florida Election Lab 2024 general (~156.8 million ballots, VEP ~244.7 million); Census CPS Voting and Registration for November 2024 (~174 million registered, 73.6% of the citizen voting-age population). Ladder now includes registered voters.

### 5.3 The Billionaire's Ceiling [C]
A billionaire could put a billion dollars into the pool and get no more value than anyone else. Their money feeds the larger pool, and everybody redistributes it. It amplifies what they like a little, by their share of the whole, but the rest still flows through everyone else's choices. You can't buy the narrative.

- Theme: Power and Representation · Links: VZ §9.3

### 5.4 Taxes That Steer [N]
We can tax the things that pollute and destroy, and give incentives for the things that build and protect. Tax is a steering mechanism, not just a reason for government to exist. The question for every dollar is whether it builds something that makes next year cheaper, or gets thrown away. We don't have to give in to what the biggest donors want. We can innovate in policy.

- Theme: Power and Representation · Links: VZ §5, §8 · Sector: Government, Politics

## Theme 6: Community and Trust

### 6.1 The Penny Economy [C]
You sign up for something like a monthly subscription, say twenty dollars, or set it to whatever you want. All your activity through the month, the likes and reboosts, decides where a portion of it goes. People who create good content that a lot of people like get money back. But ten percent of that, they automatically redistribute to things they like. That creates velocity: money that keeps moving and grows over time. It's monetizing for good.

- Theme: Community and Trust · Links: VZ §9.2, §9.4
- Note: this was a gap beat, closed by VZ §9.

### 6.2 The Vouching Network [C]
To keep someone from making a thousand accounts, there's a physical piece. Like in *Snow Crash*, you meet in person, trade encryption keys, and vouch for each other as real. Each person vouches for a handful of others. A thousand sock puppets vouching for each other form a closed clique that never connects to a real person, so their weight collapses. Real people connect outward, and their signal compounds.

- Theme: Community and Trust · Links: VZ §9.6, §9.5
- Note: this was a gap beat, closed by VZ §9.

### 6.3 The Fringe Is Open [R]
We don't want to silence anyone. Stories can live on the fringe. They just carry less credibility until the community moves them toward the center. Not every story has to be happy or life-changing. Art can give us anti-patterns. Someone might do one little thing and get a real amount of money in a flash in the pan, and someone else might love the work and keep doing more.

- Theme: Community and Trust · Links: VZ §9.7, §9.8
